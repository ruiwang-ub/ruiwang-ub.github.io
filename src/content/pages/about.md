Welcome to my website! I’m Rui Wang, an assistant professor in the Department of Communication and Media at Loyola University Maryland. I earned my Ph.D. in communication from the University at Buffalo and my M.A. in communication from Rutgers University. One of my favorite parts of this job is taking a question from everyday life, studying it, and bringing it into the classroom.

I’m curious about what happens between asking a question online and deciding what to believe. My research examines how AI and digital platforms shape the information people encounter and trust, and how political identities influence the way people engage with that information. I love collaborating with scholars in communication and across disciplines. Seeing a problem from different perspectives often leads us to new questions and gives us more ways to answer them. Here are a few projects we’re working on now.

- **Auditing AI search.** We compare 16 AI search models to see how question wording and a user’s disclosed political identity relate to the news outlets the models cite.
- **How readers judge AI answers.** We’re designing an experiment to see how readers evaluate an AI-generated answer when its position and cited sources align with or challenge their views.
- **AI disclosure and human oversight.** We study whether explaining the roles of AI and human reviewers changes how credible people find news articles and fact-checks.
- **Political identity and disagreement on Reddit.** We analyze discussions about abortion across Reddit communities to understand how people express political identities and respond to disagreement.

My research has appeared in *Political Communication*, *Communication Research*, *Computers in Human Behavior*, *New Media & Society*, *Information, Communication & Society*, *Science Communication*, *Social Media + Society*, *Social Science Computer Review*, and the *Journal of Information Technology & Politics*, among others.

As a scholar-teacher, I see teaching as a collaboration with students. I’ve taught undergraduate and graduate courses in journalism, communication, digital media, and emerging technologies. I bring questions from my research into the classroom and invite students to explore them through discussion and hands-on projects. We might trace an AI-generated answer to its sources and debate what the evidence supports, or interview users and revise a prototype based on their feedback. I love seeing a class project spark a question that a student wants to keep exploring on their own.
